# iikb

An iOS custom keyboard (UIKit, Swift 6, iOS 26+) that types like Apple's and looks like Raycast's (docs/design.md). MIT. It was called Hex until Nov 3 2026: what people see says iikb (lowercase always; `brand/BRAND.md`, `copy/MESSAGING.md`), while code names and IDs keep Hex (`HexKeyboard`, `HexStyle`, `com.shahidy.hexkeyboard`, `hexkeyboard://`), so installs keep their data.

## Layout

- `Packages/HexKeyboard/Sources/KeyboardCore`: pure Swift, no Foundation or UIKit (it also compiles to WebAssembly). Everything the keyboard does lives here, so the extension and the web demo share it. Fully unit tested.
  - `Keys/`: layouts per device class, `KeyGrid` (key frames and hit testing), metrics, key faces and theme. `Keys/Themes.swift`: Raycast's themes (Default, Clear, Physical, Mechanical, iOS 5; docs/design.md, "Themes"), each a `KeyPaint` per kind of key, made by `KeyboardTheme.named`.
  - `Keys/Emoji.swift`: `EmojiCatalog` (the bar's emoji for a word, or else for its singular; read from `Resources/emoji.txt`, picked by hand per word, where a word can also find none, and checked by `scripts/emoji.py`, which prints each word's candidates from CLDR, emojilib and gemoji), `EmojiSearch` (every emoji in Apple's order with its skin tones and CLDR names and keywords, for the emoji panel; `Resources/emoji-search.txt`, written by `scripts/emoji-search.py`) and `EmojiHistory` (the emoji you picked from the bar or the panel, and the skin tone picked for each; each pick outweighs the ones before it, no clock).
  - `Keys/Toolbar.swift`: what sits above the keys per device (`KeyboardMetrics.toolbar`): the bar (the tools sit at its left on every screen, with no row of their own), a panel's results and search row, and which edges go nowhere.
  - `Typing/Panel.swift`: the panels (emoji, GIF, clipboard). `TypingEngine.panel` is the open one and `query` its search, which the keys type into while it's open (`KeyboardEvent.panel`).
  - `Typing/SourceFeed.swift`: what one source is sent (`FieldUpdate`s, one at a time, your typing collapsed into the newest text while every change the app makes goes in turn, and what an unreachable source missed kept for your next change) and the hint it answered with.
  - `Typing/`: `TypingEngine` takes `KeyboardEvent`s (`handle`) and does shift, caps lock, autocap, double-space period, smart punctuation, layers, word delete, autocorrect and reverts, and the tap labels it learns from. `Lines` moves the caret up and down lines through a `LineLayout`, keeping its x across a short line as a text view does. It reads the text only from `TextState` (see Rules).
  - `Correction/`: `Corrector` (what to correct, text replacements, names, learned words) over a `CandidateSource`. With a `Lexicon` (a flat byte trie of words and frequencies) as its source it decodes taps (`Decoder`, exact A* over the trie) against your `TouchModel` (each key a 2D Gaussian with a conjugate Normal-Inverse-Wishart prior, whose center blends three memories of where you aim, all taps, the last ~30 and the last ~3, weighted by how well each predicted your taps) and a `LanguageModel` (Witten-Bell over your next-word counts, your words and the lexicon). A run whose spaces went astray, a space missed ("greatday"), hit as a letter on the row above the space bar ("freatvday"), or a space tap meaning a letter ("a out" in a selection), is read as the words it likeliest spells, each from its own taps after the word before it and with a typo of its own (`Corrector.split`: "iblovebuoubbaby" → "I love you baby"); a word typed as one gets them in the bar only when they beat every reading of it as one known word, and never applied by the space, since a new word you coin can read as two. A word read without its taps, as one you go back to or select is, counts each tap as landing anywhere on the key it hit (`Corrector.shares`). The bar is for the text a tap on it replaces: the selection (up to 64 characters of words and spaces), or else the whole word around the caret, so a word you go back to or select gets its corrections too. `Phrases` (the corpus's likeliest next words after each common word and pair of words) sits under your next-word counts when ranking next-word predictions; autocorrect leaves it out, because a table of each context's top words would push every other word down. `KeyMap` (which key a touch means) is made for the text as it is after every edit, so the next touch, rollover included, reads it at once; it costs hundredths of a millisecond, since `Corrector` keeps its count of words the lexicon doesn't know up to date rather than recounting, and `TypingMemory` changes in place (`Corrector.record`, `forget`, `changeMemory`). `TypingMemory` holds everything learned and is `Codable`. `Aim` turns the touch model into what the app shows: each key's learned center and spread in points, its last 48 taps and the key the grid reads each as, where each of its three memories centers and its weight, and the share of its taps the grid would put on each key.
- `Packages/HexKeyboard/Sources/KeyboardUI`: UIKit. `HexKeyboardViewController` (the extension's principal class: forwards events to the engine, renders, runs ranking jobs), `KeyboardView` (one view owns all touches: rollover, slide-off, callouts, long-press accents, spacebar trackpad, iPad flicks), `HostLayout` (where the host likely wraps its text, so the trackpad moves up and down lines: iOS tells a keyboard only the text around the caret, never where its lines wrap, so it wraps the text in the body font as wide as the screen within the system's margins, the widest any field can be), `KeyView`, `SuggestionBar` (Apple's three suggestions, the last giving way to the word's emoji when it has some, as many as fit and the rest a swipe away, the tools at its left, and `MicButton`, a round button always at its right end, all on the top row's keys (`BarLayout`): tap to dictate (opening the app while its microphone is off), tap again to stop, hold to turn the microphone off; the emoji panel opens from the emoji key beside space on iPhone, which Settings can take away), `PanelView` (a panel in place of the bar: a `PanelContent`'s results over a back button and a search field the keys type into; `EmojiPanel` is the emoji one; `GifPanel` searches KLIPY (`Klipy`, its key in `Resources/Keys/klipy.key`, kept out of git; one request out at a time, `GifSearch`) and puts a tapped GIF on the clipboard (`GifClipboard`), since a keyboard can only type text, or drags a held one into the app's field; `ClipboardPanel` shows what `ClipboardStore` kept of what you copied (in the App Group, for the app's Clipboard tab, with a copy in the keyboard's own container that a group without a history takes), read when the keyboard appears and when the panel opens, on a queue of its own, never the main thread, which iOS refuses silently unless Settings › Apps › iikb › Paste from Other Apps is Allow). The top row takes taps over the bar's bottom edge (docs/design.md, "Where taps land"), so a reach for it types its key, never picking a suggestion or opening a panel, `Lexicon.english` (`Resources/en.lexicon`, built by `scripts/lexicon`) and `Phrases.english` (`Resources/en.phrases`, built by `scripts/phrases` from the same corpus's 2- and 3-grams; sources and licenses in `Resources/LEXICON-LICENSE`), `SettingsStore` (the App Group, plus each side's own copy, so settings outlive the group's container, which iOS deletes whenever an install leaves no app claiming the group, and a Darwin notification on each save, so a keyboard that's up takes the app's changes at once) and `MemoryStore` (the extension's own container; each app has a keyboard of its own, so a keyboard saves only over the version its memory was read from or its own last save, never one another saved since or before its read lands, and never over a file it couldn't read; each keyboard view controller has its own store, since iOS makes a new one each time the keyboard comes up, often in a process whose last one already read the file; the app resets it by sharing a new reset token; with Full Access the keyboard also writes its touch model to the group as `Library/touches.json`, since a keyboard can't write the group's root, and posts a Darwin notification), `Sources` (the sources you add in the app, docs/sources.md: each gets the field you type in as JSON, off the main thread, and its hint stands in for the suggestions until tapped; only with Full Access, never password or code fields), `Dictation` (the channel to the app for voice typing: a status file in the App Group that only the app writes, which carries whether the app is recording and the length of each dictation it's still writing down, apart, and what those wait for while the model gets ready; a `Note` file for each dictation written down, or something to tell you, which the keyboard takes by removing it, so the two never write the same file and none is typed twice; a dictation is typed only by the keyboard you stopped it in, which sends a number it picks each time it appears in `finish`'s and `stop`'s state, so it never lands in a field you've left, and one whose keyboard closed first says it's in Transcripts; Darwin notifications both ways, the microphone's level and the seconds recorded so far carried in a notification's state, so the bar's time is the recording's own length; and a file lock the app holds while its microphone stays on, which the kernel drops when the app ends), `KeyboardHost` (the app the keyboard is in, shared by sources, app-specific language and emoji learning, GIF recents and the voice-typing return link, from UIKit's keyboard arbiter, which the C target `KeyboardArbiter` switches on as the keyboard loads), `KeyHaptics` (Apple's keyboard haptic values, played with public Core Haptics on an engine the keyboard owns; while any app records iOS mutes every app's haptics unless the recording session allows them, so `Listener` does, or Keep Mic On silences the keys), `KeyboardPreviewView` (Hex as some settings make it look, drawn by the keyboard's own views, for the app's preview) and `KeycapSwatchView` (a theme's A and B keys, for its picker), `TapMapView` (the keyboard drawn with what the touch model learned, for the app: Heat, Aim, Accuracy) and `TapCharts` (the heat ramp, a key's close-up and the thumbs' pull arrows).

- `App/`: the containing app, in Raycast's look (`HexStyle`, `Components`: black pages, near-black cards, radio marks; docs/design.md, "The app"), with titles and big numbers in Helvetica Neue (`HexStyle.display`, `Font.display`; `brand/BRAND.md`), in five tabs in the system tab bar, which iOS 27.1 puts in the iPhone Duo's vertical bar: iikb (whether the keyboard is on, the microphone's switch beside the title (on keeps it on until you turn it off there; off lets it go once what's said is written down; `Listener.turnMicOn`, `turnMicOff`), a field to try it, the setup switches iOS keeps until they're on, cards for today's dictation and your taps, and one for each setting reached for most, Dictionary first, opening its page in Settings), Transcripts (`TranscriptsPage`: `Transcripts` keeps every dictation in the app's own container, with its sound as a `Recording`, a 16 kHz WAV written as it's heard (a recording an ended app left with no transcript is written down on the next launch), up to 512 MB, and the microphone it came from; the page lists them with a words-a-day chart, and a transcript with its sound can be written again with the model picked now), Clipboard (`ClipboardPage`: the history the keyboard's clipboard panel keeps, the same `ClipboardStore`, pinned first then by day; a tap copies an item again, and the app keeps what's on the clipboard when it comes forward once the keyboard has started the history), Your Taps (`TapsPage`: `TapMapView` (Heat, Aim, Accuracy, laid out by `TapBoard`) with your numbers, a picked key's close-up, the thumbs' pull and misses by key, per screen of the Duo) and Settings (`SettingsPage`: every setting, in one list of pages under Keyboard (Theme, Layout, Typing, Sound & Haptics, each over a `KeyboardPreviewView` to try; `KeyboardSettingsPages`), Voice Typing (Voice Model, Writing Style, Dictionary, Keep Mic On; `VoiceSettingsPages`) and iikb (Sources, Keyboard Access, Privacy & Data, About; `HexSettingsPages`)). Each page is laid out by the size it gets, never the fold (`Arrangement`, `Panes`): the open Duo in landscape, two panes splitting the width in half; everywhere else, one column. `Router` opens a tab and settings page from another. `Settings.bundle` has the keyboard's switches in Settings › Apps › iikb. `scripts/sim duoapp` screenshots every screen of it on the Duo's four screens. Voice typing is two steps that never wait on each other, joined by `Listener`: the `Microphone` records a dictation as a `Recording` the moment the keyboard asks (by opening `hexkeyboard://dictate`, or with its start signal while the microphone is kept on), through Apple's voice processing, which hears you on all the microphones as a call does (on one, the folded Duo in portrait lost seconds of speech at a time); once you stop, `VoiceModel.write` writes the finished recording down with the model you pick in Settings: Parakeet v3 (the default) or Parakeet Ultra through FluidAudio, on the Neural Engine, downloaded once from Hugging Face, or Apple's SpeechTranscriber. Apple's never stands in for a Parakeet: a dictation made while the picked Parakeet gets ready (after an install, its Neural Engine compile takes 30 s to over 2 minutes) is recorded as any other and written once it's ready, the next one can be recorded meanwhile, and they're typed in the order said. Transcribe Again and a recording an ended app left, written as soon as the app starts, go through the same `write`. v3 is loaded when the microphone turns on and let go once it's off with nothing left to write, since it loads again in 0.1 s; Ultra, whose every load takes minutes, is kept, and the simulator, which has no Neural Engine, never loads one. Each transcript keeps the model that wrote it. What it wrote is typed as Settings › Writing Style writes it (`DictationWriting`, KeyboardCore): edited (`DictationEditing`: As Said, the default, keeps every word, um and uh included, as Bassim's said keeps them on purpose; Light leaves out um and uh; Clean also leaves out short words said twice and what "scratch that" takes back), in a `DictationStyle`, Wispr Flow's four (Formal., the default, ends with a period; Casual drops the last period and the comma after an opening "so" or "also"; very casual is Casual in lowercase; Excited! ends with "!"), and with a list you speak ("one, eggs, two, milk", or first, second) written as a numbered one (`SpokenList`, the Lists switch), since Parakeet takes no instructions and writes a spoken list as a sentence. Its capitals are the keyboard's, set where it goes in (`TypingEngine.fitted`) from the field and Settings › Keyboard › Typing › Auto-Capitalization, as typing's are, and the style (`DictationWriting.capitals`): with Auto-Capitalization off or in very casual, each sentence's first word is lowered where the lexicon spells it lowercase, and so is I, while names keep theirs; otherwise sentences start with a capital as the field asks. Then it goes through your `Vocabulary` (KeyboardCore; `VocabularyView` in Settings, kept as vocabulary.json in the app's container, so a person's words never reach git): the words the model gets wrong, each with what it writes instead, matched as whole words only, and the transcript keeps what the model wrote when the style or vocabulary changed it. A Parakeet reads the recording leveled (`Recording.leveled`: FluidAudio's Parakeets drop whole stretches of quiet speech) and held to the Latin alphabet (v3 otherwise drifts into Cyrillic). `scripts/voicebench` scores a change to that path on Bassim's own dictations. On the iPhone Duo (iOS 27.2) v3's encoder compiles for the Neural Engine in about 37 s once per install and loads in 0.1 s after; Ultra's int8 encoder never caches there and takes 3 to 6 minutes, hot, every load (`scripts/voiceprobe`; FluidAudio #1022). Parakeet Unified, the English Parakeet Anomaly's Hex uses, gets 15% fewer of Bassim's words wrong than v3 (2.06% to 2.41% with um and uh set aside) but isn't offered: in FluidAudio 0.18.0 on the Duo it never runs on the Neural Engine, taking 77 s (int8) or 51 s (fp16) to load after a first compile of 5 to 8 minutes, and 9 to 16 s to write 37 s of talking, where v3 takes 0.05 s. `HostApp` sends you back to the app the keyboard named, unless that's Hex itself, which iOS would quit, losing a Parakeet still getting ready; then it keeps the microphone on for Keep Mic On so the next dictation starts from the keyboard without opening the app.
- `App/`: the containing app, in Raycast's look (`HexStyle`, `Components`: black pages, near-black cards, radio marks; docs/design.md, "The app"), with titles and big numbers in Helvetica Neue (`HexStyle.display`, `Font.display`; `brand/BRAND.md`), in five tabs in the system tab bar, which iOS 27.1 puts in the iPhone Duo's vertical bar: iikb (whether the keyboard is on, a field to try it, the setup switches iOS keeps until they're on, cards for today's dictation and your taps, and one for each setting reached for most, Dictionary first, opening its page in Settings), Transcripts (`TranscriptsPage`: `Transcripts` keeps every dictation in the app's own container, with its sound as a `Recording`, a 16 kHz WAV written as it's heard (a recording an ended app left with no transcript is written down on the next launch), up to 512 MB, and the microphone it came from; the page lists them with a words-a-day chart, and a transcript with its sound can be written again with the model picked now), Clipboard (`ClipboardPage`: the history the keyboard's clipboard panel keeps, the same `ClipboardStore`, pinned first then by day; a tap copies an item again, and the app keeps what's on the clipboard when it comes forward once the keyboard has started the history), Your Taps (`TapsPage`: `TapMapView` (Heat, Aim, Accuracy, laid out by `TapBoard`) with your numbers, a picked key's close-up, the thumbs' pull and misses by key, per screen of the Duo) and Settings (`SettingsPage`: every setting, in one list of pages under Keyboard (Theme, Layout, Typing, Sound & Haptics, each over a `KeyboardPreviewView` to try; `KeyboardSettingsPages`), Voice Typing (Voice Model, Writing Style, Dictionary, Keep Mic On; `VoiceSettingsPages`) and iikb (Sources, Keyboard Access, Privacy & Data, About; `HexSettingsPages`)). Each page is laid out by the size it gets, never the fold (`Arrangement`, `Panes`): the open Duo in landscape, two panes splitting the width in half; everywhere else, one column. `Router` opens a tab and settings page from another. `Settings.bundle` has the keyboard's switches in Settings › Apps › iikb. `scripts/sim duoapp` screenshots every screen of it on the Duo's four screens. Voice typing is two steps that never wait on each other, joined by `Listener`: the `Microphone` records a dictation as a `Recording` the moment the keyboard asks (by opening `hexkeyboard://dictate`, or with its start signal while the microphone is kept on), through Apple's voice processing, which hears you on all the microphones as a call does (on one, the folded Duo in portrait lost seconds of speech at a time); once you stop, `VoiceModel.write` writes the finished recording down with the model you pick in Settings: Parakeet v3 (the default) or Parakeet Ultra through FluidAudio, on the Neural Engine, downloaded once from Hugging Face, or Apple's SpeechTranscriber. Apple's never stands in for a Parakeet: a dictation made while the picked Parakeet gets ready (after an install, its Neural Engine compile takes 30 s to over 2 minutes) is recorded as any other and written once it's ready, the next one can be recorded meanwhile, and they're typed in the order said. Transcribe Again and a recording an ended app left, written as soon as the app starts, go through the same `write`. v3 is loaded when the microphone turns on and let go once it's off with nothing left to write, since it loads again in 0.1 s; Ultra, whose every load takes minutes, is kept, and the simulator, which has no Neural Engine, never loads one. Each transcript keeps the model that wrote it. What it wrote is typed as Settings › Writing Style writes it (`DictationWriting`, KeyboardCore): edited (`DictationEditing`: As Said, the default, keeps every word, um and uh included, as Bassim's said keeps them on purpose; Light leaves out um and uh; Clean also leaves out short words said twice, "like" and "you know" said between commas, what "scratch that" takes back and what you fix as you go, "at two, I mean three" typed "at three"), in a `DictationStyle`, Wispr Flow's four, each setting all the punctuation, never the words (every style ends a question the model wrote with a period with "?"; Formal. keeps every comma, ends every sentence, sets off an opening "However" or "Actually" and keeps one "!" or "?"; Casual, the default, drops the last period, the comma after an opening "so", "hey" or "thanks", and semicolons for commas; very casual is Casual in lowercase without the comma before "and", "but" or "so"; Excited! is Formal with "!" ending the last sentence, every short one and every cheer), and with a list you speak ("one, eggs, two, milk", or first, second) written as a numbered or bulleted one, or left in its sentence (`SpokenList`, `DictationLists`, Settings › Lists), since Parakeet takes no instructions and writes a spoken list as a sentence. Its capitals are the keyboard's, set where it goes in (`TypingEngine.fitted`) from the field and Settings › Keyboard › Typing › Auto-Capitalization, as typing's are, and the style (`DictationWriting.capitals`): with Auto-Capitalization off or in very casual, each sentence's first word is lowered where the lexicon spells it lowercase, and so is I, while names keep theirs; otherwise sentences start with a capital as the field asks. Then it goes through your `Vocabulary` (KeyboardCore; `VocabularyView` in Settings, kept as vocabulary.json in the app's container, so a person's words never reach git): the words the model gets wrong, each with what it writes instead, matched as whole words only, and the transcript keeps what the model wrote when the style or vocabulary changed it. A Parakeet reads the recording leveled (`Recording.leveled`: FluidAudio's Parakeets drop whole stretches of quiet speech) and held to the Latin alphabet (v3 otherwise drifts into Cyrillic). `scripts/voicebench` scores a change to that path on Bassim's own dictations. - `UITests/`: types through the real extension in the simulator.
- Backup (docs/backup.md): `Backup` (KeyboardUI) sends iikb's files to the server built in as `Resources/Keys/backup.key` (kept out of git; `scripts/phone` won't build without it, `scripts/sync` keeps it off the Mac so simulators never send) each time they change: the keyboard as it goes away, the app as it starts and as it writes down a dictation or changes the Dictionary (`Backup.syncApp`, never a recording still being made). It sends what the server lacks or holds at another size or modification time to the second, so a failed send goes with the next. `server/backup.ts` keeps them in `~/.local/share/hex-keyboard/backup/mirror` as `scripts/backup` does, and moves a file it replaces to `versions/<time>/`, deleting nothing; `scripts/restore` puts them back, as they were at any time.
- `project.yml`: XcodeGen spec. The `.xcodeproj` is generated, never committed.

## Build and test

Development happens on a Linux box. Simulator builds, tests and screenshots run on a Mac over ssh (`ssh mac`); the iPhone build is made here and installed through the Mac with `scripts/phone` (see below). `scripts/mac <command>` syncs the tree to one of the Mac's three slots and runs the command there inside one of the Mac's turns (`turn`, two heavy runs at once across projects); each slot has its own folder and its own simulators, so up to two runs go at once and the rest wait, and the Mac never runs more than three simulators, counting other projects'. A change is checked here first: `scripts/coretest` for KeyboardCore, `scripts/check` for whether everything builds for the iPhone. Then it goes to main and onto the phone with `scripts/phone` as soon as it's ready, since the phone is where changes are tried. The simulator is for what only it shows (a layout, a screen, typing through the real extension), one run at a time, and one to three agents test what landed together with `scripts/verify`, which always tests main's last commit. Device builds take the team from `Config/Team.xcconfig` (`DEVELOPMENT_TEAM = …`, not committed), which syncing leaves in place. To prove a change works, follow the `verify` skill (`.claude/skills/verify`).

Prefer committing straight to main, merge parallel work like a zipper on new projects

```sh
scripts/mac scripts/build                       # sync to the Mac, xcodegen, build for the simulator
scripts/coretest                                # KeyboardCore's tests here on Linux, no Mac (about 25 s)
scripts/check                                   # this checkout built for the iPhone here on Linux, unsigned (a few minutes; one at a time)
scripts/phone                                   # main's last commit built, signed and installed on the iPhone (--reinstall renews the signing)
scripts/backup <udid>                           # the iPhone's iikb data copied to ~/.local/share/hex-keyboard/backup here, never deleted, replaced files kept in versions/ (scripts/phone runs it first)
scripts/restore <udid> [--at <time>] [name…]    # the backup put back on the iPhone, all of it or the files named, as it is or as it was at a time (docs/backup.md)
scripts/mac scripts/verify                      # what landed, at once, on one iPhone Duo simulator: package and UI tests, light and dark, shots in shots/raw/verify/
scripts/mac scripts/test                        # KeyboardCore and KeyboardUI package tests on an iOS simulator
scripts/mac scripts/uitest                      # type through the real extension (the iPhone Duo by default)
scripts/mac 'scripts/sim all v7'                # screenshots: system vs Hex, light and dark, 8 devices
scripts/mac 'scripts/sim variants "iPhone 17" v7'  # return key types, keyboard types, landscape
scripts/mac 'scripts/sim duo v8'                # Apple's keyboard and Hex on the iPhone Duo's four screens, folded and open (unfolded with Device Hub's hinge event, scripts/devicehub.c)
scripts/mac 'scripts/capture "iPhone 17" iPhone-17 hex'  # frames of held keys: pop-ups, accent panel (CAPTURE=menu: the 123 menu)
scripts/mac 'scripts/shots "iPhone 17" iPhone-17 system light "hel:hel|helo:o"'  # type through a keyboard, screenshot each step
scripts/mac 'scripts/sim taps "iPhone 17 Pro Max" iPhone-17-Pro-Max touches.json t1'  # the app's Your Taps screen with a touch model
scripts/mac scripts/sourcetest                  # a source through the real keyboard, scripts/source.py standing in for it; prints every update it was sent
scripts/mac scripts/voicetest                   # the bar's mic through the real keyboard, scripts/voiceapp.py standing in for the app; screenshots light and dark
scripts/voiceprobe v3 clip.wav                  # on the iPhone: a Parakeet's load and write times in the installed Hex (scripts/phone)
```

`scripts/sim` creates its own `hexkb<slot> …` simulators and enables the extension by writing `AppleKeyboards` (and, for iPad, `AppleKeyboardsExpanded = 1`) into the simulator's global preferences, so no Settings UI is needed. Simulators boot headless; the Simulator app never opens. Reference screenshots of Apple's keyboard come from the same simulators with `KEYBOARDS=system`.

Builds are ad-hoc signed for the simulator (`CODE_SIGN_IDENTITY = -`), which embeds simulated entitlements, so the App Group works in the simulator without a team. Device builds need a team with the group `group.com.shahidy.hexkeyboard` registered.

`scripts/phone` builds the app and keyboard with [xtool](https://github.com/xtool-org/xtool) from the root `Package.swift` and `xtool.yml` (their Info.plists are `Config/xtool/`, kept in step with `project.yml` by hand), signs them with a free-team profile that xtool renews on every run (it covers every device the team has registered; a new phone gets registered by one direct `xtool install` to it), stamps main's commit count as the build number (Settings › About shows it; an iPhone that already has that build is left alone), and installs them through the Mac with `devicectl` on the iPhone the Mac reaches, by cable or Wi-Fi, once no recording there was written in the last 10 seconds, so an install never cuts off a dictation, then starts Hex in the background, so Parakeet's compile for the new install begins then rather than at the next dictation. It needs Swift 6.4, xtool built from [AVGVSTVS96/xtool](https://github.com/AVGVSTVS96/xtool) (it signs with the real bundle and group IDs, so it updates the installed app and keeps its data, where stock xtool installs a separate `XTL-…` copy; and it keeps the App Group on free teams, which stock xtool drops, so iOS deletes the group's container), xtool's Darwin SDK (`xtool sdk install --slim` on an Xcode whose iPhoneOS platform also holds Xcode-beta's `iPhoneOS27.1.sdk`: the fork picks the newest iPhoneOS SDK for `arm64-apple-ios` and puts `-Xclang-linker -isysroot -Xclang-linker <its path>` in the bundle's `toolset-swb.json`; never rebuild xtool while others build, since a rebuild that changes its Anisette provider logs it out), `xtool auth login`, and the iPhone paired with the Mac. The keyboard target links with `-ObjC` because its principal class is only named in Info.plist. On Linux the Swift driver hands clang `--sysroot`, and clang reads an SDK's version only from `-isysroot`, so without that option the binaries record the deployment target (26.0) as their SDK; iOS 27.1 then runs Hex on the iPhone Duo as an old iPhone app, small and without the vertical bar, which the simulator never showed. `scripts/phone` refuses a build linked below 27.1, and `scripts/mac` runs everything on the Mac with Xcode-beta (27.1) so the simulator shows what the phone does.

`scripts/eval` types the MacKenzie & Soukoreff phrase set (500 phrases, 2,714 words) through `TypingEngine` with taps drawn from a Gaussian around the real key centers (`KeyGrid`, iPhone 17 width), seeded, and prints word accuracy without and with autocorrect and the top-3 suggestion hit rate, for three keyboards: (a) nearest key + `UITextChecker`, (b) Hex's decoder with the touch model held at its prior, (c) the decoder learning. `noise` reruns the original noise levels; `biasedThumbs` types the corpus twice as a user whose m, b and o land off target and prints a learning curve; `aimShift` types it once that way, then again after the user's aim moves (lower and right, o and e swinging the other way), and prints how fast the learning keyboard follows; `missedSpaces` types two, three and four words in a row from each phrase with every space's tap landing on the row above the space bar, and two and three with the spaces never typed, and prints how often the key map reads a tap as the space anyway, and how often the bar offers the words when it reads none; `predictions` scores the suggestion bar before each word (is the next word in the top 3, is it first) on the phrase set and on `chat.txt` (1,000 sentences from Taskmaster-1 user turns), for a new user and one whose memory keeps every phrase. It runs a Release build (the decoder is slow in Debug), here on Linux in a package of KeyboardCore, the word lists and `Evaluation` alone, which gives the same numbers as the Mac; only the baseline needs `UITextChecker`, so its lines are scored on the Mac (`scripts/mac scripts/eval`). A full run is diffed against `scripts/eval.expected`: a change that moves the numbers must beat them, and updates that file in the same commit.

## Rules

- Keys behave as Apple's do (layouts, pop-ups, accents, the trackpad, the suggestion bar) and are laid out from Apple's keyboard measured in the same simulator (`shots/`, `scripts/shotdiff.py`), never eyeballed. Their look and sizes are docs/design.md's, after Raycast's keyboard: where it moves keys off Apple's geometry (the bottom row's widths, the Duo's keys filling the room they're given), it says so and why; everywhere else a key sits where Apple's does.
- Behavior follows real events (touches, `textDidChange`, `selectionDidChange`). The keyboard's only timers are the inherent ones: key repeat, long-press, and a source's 10 s to answer. The app's are Keep Mic On's countdown, the second a stop waits for quiet before it cuts, and one clock reading: a Parakeet that takes over 0.5 s to write a second of silence is taken to be off the Neural Engine and loaded again, up to three times (`VoiceModel`).
- No comments unless they record a non-obvious fact.
- Keycaps use SF Compact, loaded from the system font file, because that is what the system keyboard uses (verified by glyph metrics) and UIKit has no public name for it. It falls back to SF Pro.
- iOS 26 ignores a plain height constraint on the input view; the height comes from Auto Layout content (`keyboardHeightConstraint`).
- A keyboard can't use the microphone (no access, even with Full Access), so voice typing opens the app, which records in the background (`UIBackgroundModes` audio) after starting in the foreground, and hands the text back through the App Group. Apps can't be opened from a keyboard through public API; Hex walks the responder chain to the host's `UIApplication`, as Wispr Flow and others do. Since iOS 26.4 a keyboard's own properties don't name the app you were typing in, and a keyboard can't reopen it, so `KeyboardHost` matches its host's process ID to the app UIKit's keyboard arbiter names, names it in the link, and the app opens it once the microphone is on, with `LSApplicationWorkspace` (all private) or else the app's own link; if that fails its screen asks you to swipe back. No Live Activity is needed for this, and Hex starts none: iOS shows its own mic indicator. Once recording, the app may keep the microphone running in the background (Keep Mic On, up to Until Turned Off, as Wispr Flow's Flow Sessions do), and while it does the mic beside the suggestions starts and stops dictation with Darwin notifications, never opening the app.
- Third-party keyboards get a system bottom bar (globe, mic) on Face ID iPhones and a system undo/redo/paste bar on iPad. Metrics are calibrated so the keys land where Apple's do with those bars in place.
- A third-party keyboard can't draw above its own area, so top-row pop-ups are clamped into the space above the keys, where they draw over the bar. iOS draws the keyboard's backdrop 4pt above the keyboard's own top, so on iPhone the bar is 38pt under 3pt of padding, 7pt from the backdrop's top and 7pt from the keys, with the tools beside the suggestions rather than in a row of their own (`Toolbar`): on the iPhone Duo the keyboard is as tall as Apple's with its suggestions.
- On iPad the system's own shortcut bar (undo, redo, paste) sits above a third-party keyboard and its middle can't be used, so Hex adds a 36pt suggestion row there, for the mic and, with Predictive Text on, the suggestions.
- A correctly typed real word must never be changed. The corrector replaces a word that stands on its own (kept, or read as itself when typed perfectly) only when the touches favor the other word; word frequencies may only choose among what the fingers could have meant.
- Learning comes only from real events: the letters of a word you kept once you move on (next word, the message sent, keyboard hidden), a reverted correction teaches what you typed, and a backspace-then-retype within a quarter key labels the erased tap. Words you delete teach nothing. Nothing in the model runs on a clock: older taps fade only as newer taps of the same key arrive, and every weight is a posterior or a count.
- The engine reads the text only from its `TextState`. Every edit goes through `TypingEngine.write`, which changes the host and `TextState` together; the host's reports (`textDidChange`, `selectionDidChange`) reach it only through `documentChanged`, which ignores reports that show the keyboard's own edits late or in a shorter window, the text after the caret included, and adopts anything else. Automatic edits only replace characters the keyboard typed itself (`TextState.owned`).
- iOS doesn't deliver touches that land on fully transparent pixels of a third-party keyboard. `KeyboardInputView` has an invisible fill (alpha 0.001, pixel-identical in screenshots) so gaps between keys still take taps; `FirstTapTests` checks it.
- Recording never waits for a model: the `Microphone` knows nothing of `VoiceModel`, which takes only finished recordings, and the status carries recording and writing apart, so nothing can make a dictation wait to start.
- Keyboard behavior goes in `KeyboardCore`; `KeyboardUI` and `web/` only draw, play feedback and forward events, so the two never drift.
- `KeyboardCore` takes no Foundation or UIKit import (`ArchitectureTests` checks). Platform services (UILexicon, files, the scorer's UITextChecker) live outside it behind Core protocols.

Host identity is keyboard-wide context: `KeyboardHost` resolves it on appearance and UIKit text/selection events; sources receive its bundle ID (`app`, empty when unknown), language ranking learns per-app word and next-word counts over shared vocabulary, emoji choices and GIF recents prefer this app’s picks, and voice typing uses it to return. Counts and picks stay local; the GIF service receives no host ID. App identity cannot establish clipboard origin or a conversation.
